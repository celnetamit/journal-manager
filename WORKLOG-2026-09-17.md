## What was built, not only fixed

* **A report for the author** — what the manuscript does not contain, and the peer
  review cut to Summary / Major Concerns / Minor Issues / Recommendation.
* **Author-addressed comments** — `Query to Author`, with a bold `Author:` label. The
  internal redline still says `AI Editor`.
* **Subject area** — reported with its agreement. At journal-topic level the top three
  recommendations agree 42% of the time; at domain level, 83%.

## The pattern in today's failures

Four of the ten were *my own guards*, and all four failed the same way: **they were
given the wrong list and reported success either way.**

* the reference-loss guard compared entries by first-author-and-year, an identity a
  reformat changes;
* the recasing guard was handed the cells worth copyediting, not the cells;
* the citation guard fixed the sentence and left the numbering it had shifted;
* the gap-marker coloured runs, after the diff had cut the marker across three of them.

A guard that cannot see its input is not a guard. Two things now follow from that and
were applied to everything shipped today: **measure on the corpus before wiring it in**,
and **run the new test against a broken version first** — a check that has never failed
is not known to work.
