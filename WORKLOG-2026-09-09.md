## The four that were losing the author's work

These are the ones that mattered. In each, content left the manuscript and nothing said
so — the report looked clean.

**Three of four figures were deleted (job #53).** `_mark_up_paragraph` calls
`Paragraph.clear()` to rewrite a paragraph, and python-docx's `clear()` removes every
child element, including the `w:drawing` that *is* the figure. The pattern was exact:
every paragraph whose text was edited lost its picture, and the one image-bearing
paragraph the copyedit left alone kept its own. Authors write a figure and its caption
in one paragraph, so copyediting the caption destroyed the figure it captioned. The
file still carried image1-3 in `word/media/` with their relationships intact,
referenced by nothing.
→ Pictures are lifted out before the clear and put back after.

**Both numbered elements lost their labels (job #53).** `Table 2: Comparative Summary`
→ `Comparative Summary`, because "Table 2:" reads exactly like the heading numbering
rule 2 removes; and `3.2 Sensor Fusion    Fig: 2 Design of Prototype` → `Sensor
Fusion`, where a heading and a caption shared a paragraph and only the heading
survived. The manuscript then failed its own cross-reference check for captions the
tool had deleted.
→ A caption's number is restored, and the heading rules now say in words that a caption
is not a heading.

**Four references were replaced by copies of others (job #62).** Sixteen entries in,
sixteen out, every one well-formed and correctly converted to Vancouver — and UNESCO's
OER Recommendation, its 2026 restatement, Vygotsky & Cole (1978) and Almasri (2024)
were gone, with Seale, Wang, Tlili and Hamilton each appearing twice. Vygotsky is
argued from by name in the body, so the paper cited a source it no longer listed.
→ Two failures underneath: the re-sort's census fingerprinted entries by their
*wording*, and two differently-reformatted copies of one work counted as two distinct
works; and the block guard ran in the middle of the chain, leaving every later step
unguarded. Identity is now surname + year, and the question is asked again immediately
before the redline.

**A rejected copyedit threw away its spelling corrections (job #53).** ¶68 held a
heading, a source note, a citation and a body sentence in one paragraph; the edit
shortened it by 37%, the loss guard restored the original, and `comparision` →
`comparison` and `breaking` → `braking` went back with it.
→ The revert is partial on terms that cannot lose anything: every original token is
kept unless a single word was replaced by a single, recognisably similar word.

## Rules that were doing nothing

**`Information and Communication Technology` spelled IaCT.** Initials were taken from
every word, so any acronym skipping an "and" or an "of" was never learned — and an
abbreviation the guard has not learned is one it does nothing about. That is how ICT
was expanded seven times and OER eight with the first-use rule switched on and
reporting itself as applied. UNESCO was missed the same way (a comma in the name).

**`proportional-integral-derivative (PID)` defined nothing**, for the same reason on
hyphens — which hid it from every check that starts from "what does this paper define",
including the abstract one.

**Both reference guards looked for a heading matching exactly `References`.** This
manuscript writes `References:`. With the colon they found no bibliography and did
nothing at all, silently.

**A web reference's link was required and unguarded.** Job #61 returned
`Unesco.org. 2026. Available from:` with the address gone.

## Things nothing was looking at

- **Duplicate references.** Job #53 listed Aggarwal's book as entries 6 and 8. Every
  check read one entry at a time, and each is well-formed alone.
- **`CONCETRATION (M)`** across the top of Table 2 (job #52). The copyeditor is only
  given cells with three or more words — deliberately, so it is never handed a bare
  number to "correct" — and the proofreader only ever saw body text.
- **Superscript reference markers** (job #52). `read_docx` returns `p.text`, so
  `…was allowed to react` with a superscript 45 arrived as `react45`: not a citation
  any rule could recognise.
- **An equation cited but never numbered** (job #52). Figures and tables were
  cross-checked; equations were not, because their number is a bracket at the end of a
  line rather than a caption at the front.
- **DOIs appended to the literature review** (job #53). The test was "over 30
  characters and contains a year", which is most of a discussion section.
- **Word's own heading numbers** (job #60). The copyedit removes typed heading numbers
  correctly; "1. Introduction" was never in the text. Removal is decided per numbering
  *definition* — job #60 has 76 numbered paragraphs across 18 definitions and `numId=1`
  holds exactly the four section headings.

## Where a check was wrong and was cut back

Two are worth remembering, because in both cases the first version passed its own tests
and was wrong on the real manuscript.

- **Missing-field reporting** started with title, journal and publisher. Measured
  against job #61's sixteen references it fired five times; reading all five by hand
  showed four were wrong (`The UDL Guidelines` is a title; `Legal Instruments` is a
  source). The two unreliable arms were removed rather than tuned.
- **The journal-abbreviation check** took the majority of the list as the convention.
  A manuscript arriving with journal names spelled out — the ordinary case, and the
  reason In-House Reference Rule 2 exists — has a spelled-out majority, so on exactly
  those it told the editor to undo the house rule. The direction is now the rule's.
  (Majority belongs to the language decision, US/UK, and is untouched there.)

## Rules review

All eleven in-house rules read against every deterministic guard. One real conflict
(above), two rules the code never enforced (caption-is-not-a-heading, the web link).

Two ambiguities are **decisions for the team**, left alone deliberately:

1. **Abbreviations in the keywords line.** The rule says only the first keyword is
   capitalised "except proper nouns". `ICT tools` is an abbreviation, not a proper
   noun. Current behaviour keeps the capitals, which looks right and is not written
   down.
2. **Is the keywords line part of the abstract?** The abstract rule forbids
   abbreviations. Job #61's keywords kept `ICT tools`. Both readings are defensible.
